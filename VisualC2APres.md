## Before

### Jack
Let’s talk about Jack first. As a STEM student, an avid trail runner, and someone living with his long-term partner, Jack lives a balanced, typical life. He has a solid circle of close friends, and his family doesn’t live too far away. 

Like most of us, Jack uses social media normally—nobody would look at him and call him addicted to his phone. He and his partner often share photos together on Instagram after big events or fun dates. After a long trail run or hike, Jack always posts his activity on Strava—often with a great view from the climb. He’s fully engaged when hanging out with friends, only pulling out Instagram or Facebook in dead time, like waiting in line for a burrito. After a long day of classes and a shift at work, he often plops down on the couch to doomscroll Reels or TikTok.

Lately, though, Jack has been carrying a low hum of anxiety and mental fatigue he can’t quite explain. He blames his schedule—extra hours at work, upcoming finals, and stress over AI shaking up his future career. So he goes for a long run to try to reset, and spends his evening on the couch scrolling to decompress. Yet the next morning, he still lacks motivation and feels scattered and restless. He thinks about texting a buddy to hang out—maybe that’ll help him reset—but he hesitates because they haven’t talked in a while and that friend has been posting nonstop about a new group of friends. Jack feels small and alone, even though nothing in his life is broken; he’s completely normal.

## After

#### Jill

Now let’s talk about Jill. On paper, she has the exact same person as Jack—good grades, a seasonal internship, close friends, family, a partner, and normal ups and downs. However, unlike Jack, Jill doesn’t carry that ever-looming anxiety. She doesn’t get FOMO; in fact, she feels closer to her friends and enjoys her hobbies more than before. Every day she feels like she’s doing less, yet her grades, her employer, and her social life prove she’s accomplishing more than ever. The only difference? A few years ago, when Jill hit burnout, she walked away from social media completely—and that one choice changed everything.


### Personal Story: Jack —> Jill
Obviously, Jack and Jill are fictional characters, but their stories are not. Just a few years ago, I was Jack—overwhelmed, drained, and feeling left out—and I made that crazy decision to delete everything: Instagram, Snapchat, YouTube Shorts, anything that could steal my time away from me. At first, it felt like a mistake, and even today people want to connect with me on Instagram or Facebook. Stepping away from social media has also helped me learn more about myself. I actually make conversation with people waiting in line for a burrito now—and when someone asks for my Instagram, explaining why I walked away almost always turns into a real conversation. I’ve never felt more connected to my friends or my own life.
